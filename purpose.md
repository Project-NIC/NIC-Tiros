# NIC-Tiros — purpose (level 1)

**What it does.** A unit that records what it goes through — acceleration, rotation, the magnetic
field, the air's pressure, temperature and humidity — into its own memory, sends a live view of it
while it can, and then tells where it is until it is found.

**For whom.** Model rocketeers and model fliers who want to know what their rocket or plane did
and to find it afterwards. It is built from ordinary parts, at a fraction of what such trackers
are sold for.

**The units.** **Baker**, the small one, for a model rocket and anything as small: a short flight
recorded whole. **Able**, the big one, for a model plane and anything that flies long: long
records, onto a card. **Ham**, on the ground: receives what Baker and Able send and shows it. Baker
and Able are one construction, **Goddard**; Ham is a bought board.

**How we know it works.**
- After a flight the whole record is in memory, and the top of a rocket's flight can be read from
  it.
- What was sent during the flight reached the ground, and a lost piece of it lost nothing, since
  the memory holds it all.
- The unit is found by the position it reports.

**What it is not.** It steers nothing: it measures and reports. It is no guidance of any kind. It
is no weather station: the standing station went to NIC-Heimdall.
